1 / EMIT

Electrons make synchrotron radiation

Relativistic electrons moving in a magnetic field emit across a broad range of radio frequencies. There is no single location where a particular observing frequency is created.

2 / ABSORB

Some radiation loses energy to the plasma

The electron population also absorbs synchrotron radiation. A packet launched upstream can be absorbed before leaving the jet; another launched farther out can escape. This is absorption along a sightline, rather than a boundary that creates photons.

3 / OBSERVE

The bright core shifts with frequency

Lower frequencies have greater synchrotron opacity in this model, hiding more of the upstream emission. Higher frequencies reveal a region nearer the engine. Switch absorption off to see how the opacity-driven shift disappears.

What is calculated · radiative transfer and the core-shift law

Emission and absorption together

The calculation uses an isotropic power-law electron population N(γ) = Kγ−p, with p = 2.4, and the standard synchrotron scalings away from spectral cutoffs:

jν ∝ K B(p+1)/2 ν−(p−1)/2
αν ∝ K B(p+2)/2 ν−(p+4)/2
dIν/dℓ = jν − ανIν

Each plotted sightline crosses a conical jet with a 4° half-opening angle. The code integrates emission and attenuation from its far boundary to its near boundary. A packet emitted on the axis has survival probability exp(−τfront), where τfront is the remaining optical depth. Individual packet stops sample this probability; they do not represent a universal absorption distance. Model background: Lobanov (1998).

Why the canonical result is r ∝ ν−1

For B ∝ r−m, K ∝ r−n, a conical jet, and a constant Doppler factor, the opacity-driven scale follows

rτ≈1 ∝ ν−1/kᵣ
kᵣ = [2n + (p+2)m − 2] / (p+4)

The default m = 1 and n = 2 give kᵣ = 1. This is a useful baseline; equipartition and a conical geometry should be tested for each source. Recent analyses generalize these scalings to other jet shapes and acceleration. Nokhrina et al. (2026).

Scope of this diagram

This is a steady, axisymmetric teaching example with arbitrary intensity and opacity normalization. A constant Doppler factor and redshift are absorbed into that normalization. It omits beam convolution, time variability, polarization, radiative cooling, and detailed particle cutoffs. Free-free absorption in surrounding thermal gas and standing shocks can also influence real radio cores. Its emission locations are independent of the core-distance sliders in the 3C 66B binary model; no fit to 3C 66B is implied.

Recent literature and evidence · reviewed 22 October 2026
Liu et al. (2026) · BL Lacertae

Multifrequency astrometry measured a shift reaching 250 μas between 8.4 and 43.2 GHz. Their kᵣ ≈ 1.18, with substantial uncertainty, is consistent with the canonical opacity model.

Nokhrina et al. (2026) · generalized jet scalings

Their self-absorbed synchrotron framework allows different jet shapes, plasma and field gradients, and Doppler-factor evolution. An inverse-frequency shift is not imposed on every AGN.

Pérez-Díez et al. (2025; arXiv 2026) · 1803+784

A measured kᵣ ≈ 0.73 challenges a simple conical equipartition interpretation for this source. The gradient controls explore how a different core-shift slope can arise.

Foundational opacity treatment: Lobanov (1998), Ultracompact jets in AGN. The interactive model is a simplified implementation of standard radiative transfer; its chosen normalization and packet animation are teaching devices.

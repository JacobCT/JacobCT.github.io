Project the motion. Draw the ellipse.

Why aren’t the ellipses concentric?

We observe the same galaxy, but each frequency traces a different emission region along the jet: 8.4 GHz sees closer to the black hole, while 2.3 GHz sees farther out. An inclined jet places these regions at different RA and Dec positions, so each ellipse has its own mean center. This is a frequency-dependent core shift. If the mean jet pointed directly toward us, the centers would project onto the same point. The 3D view retains this separation; the comparison plots below subtract each core’s mean position to compare their motions.

03 / FROM GEOMETRY TO ASTROMETRY

What is actually moving?

1

Orbit the central engine

The less massive black hole follows a circular orbit about the barycenter. The companion moves in the opposite direction, with radius q times smaller. Their sizes are symbolic.

2

Sample the radio jet

The radio core is an approximate optical-depth-one (τ ≈ 1) emission region. The 8.4 GHz core lies nearer the engine; the 2.3 GHz core lies farther out. These are emission centroids, not individual plasma particles.

3

Look along the sightline

Remove the line-of-sight coordinate. A sinusoidal 3D trajectory projects to an ellipse on the sky. Plotting its east and north components against time gives the two sinusoidal traces.

The analytical model and its limits

Orbital motion

rₘ(φ) = R [u cos φ + v sin φ]
rᴹ(φ) = −q rₘ(φ)
φ = 2π tobs / Pobs

u and v span the orbit; i is the angle between its angular momentum and the line of sight. The node angle Ω is measured east of north. We use Psrc = Pobs / (1 + z) for the orbital speed and Newtonian Kepler mass.

Jet precession: a first-order response

δj(φ) = fₚ [vₘ − (vₘ · j₀)j₀] / (βc)
rν(φ) = rₘ(φ) + sν[j₀ + δj(φ)]

The mean jet direction j₀ may be misaligned with the orbital angular momentum. Adding the orbital velocity to the jet produces periodic changes in its direction, called jet precession in the paper. Their displacement grows with core distance sν. The amplitude slider fₚ scales this transverse response: zero removes precession, and one restores the orbital-velocity estimate. The maximum instantaneous tilt from the mean jet is ψmax = arctan[fₚ vₘ/(βc)]. The direction sweep need not form a circular cone. Its period is tied to the orbit, as in the mechanism described in Figure 3; it is not an independently modelled slow precession of the black-hole spin. This is a small-angle, instantaneous kinematic illustration, not an equation supplied or fitted by Sudou et al.

Projection onto the sky

Δα = (rν − ⟨rν⟩) · E / D
Δδ = (rν − ⟨rν⟩) · N / D

E points east, N points north, and n points toward the observer. Angles are converted from radians to μas. Astronomy convention is used: east is left in the sky plots. Here Δα is the eastward tangent-plane angular offset (often written Δα cos δ). Rotating the inspection camera never changes the physical viewing angle. The projection screen is offset beyond the jet for clarity; its display distance does not change the sky coordinates.

The model is a linear combination A cos φ + B sin φ, so its projected paths are ellipses. Their semiaxes are the singular values of the 2 × 2 sky coefficient matrix.

Interpretation matters

The model omits propagation delays, relativistic velocity addition, radiative transfer, lensing, inspiral, and differential light-travel time. It does not uniquely reproduce both fitted ellipses. Changing the sliders explores the proposed mechanism; it does not infer the binary’s true parameters. The motion selector removes terms from the radio-core trajectory; the black-hole orbits remain visible as context.

Why does frequency change what we see?

1 / EMIT

Electrons make synchrotron radiation

Relativistic electrons moving in a magnetic field emit across a broad range of radio frequencies. There is no single location where a particular observing frequency is created.

2 / ABSORB

Some radiation loses energy to the plasma

The electron population also absorbs synchrotron radiation. A packet launched upstream can be absorbed before leaving the jet; another launched farther out can escape. This is absorption along a sightline, rather than a boundary that creates photons.

3 / OBSERVE

The bright core shifts with frequency

Lower frequencies have greater synchrotron opacity in this model, hiding more of the upstream emission. Higher frequencies reveal a region nearer the engine. Switch absorption off to see how the opacity-driven shift disappears.

What is calculated · radiative transfer and the core-shift law

Emission and absorption together

The calculation uses an isotropic power-law electron population N(γ) = Kγ−p, with p = 2.4, and the standard synchrotron scalings away from spectral cutoffs:

jν ∝ K B(p+1)/2 ν−(p−1)/2
αν ∝ K B(p+2)/2 ν−(p+4)/2
dIν/dℓ = jν − ανIν

Each plotted sightline crosses a conical jet with a 4° half-opening angle. The code integrates emission and attenuation from its far boundary to its near boundary. A packet emitted on the axis has survival probability exp(−τfront), where τfront is the remaining optical depth. Individual packet stops sample this probability; they do not represent a universal absorption distance. Model background: Lobanov (1998).

Why the canonical result is r ∝ ν−1

For B ∝ r−m, K ∝ r−n, a conical jet, and a constant Doppler factor, the opacity-driven scale follows

rτ≈1 ∝ ν−1/kᵣ
kᵣ = [2n + (p+2)m − 2] / (p+4)

The default m = 1 and n = 2 give kᵣ = 1. This is a useful baseline; equipartition and a conical geometry should be tested for each source. Recent analyses generalize these scalings to other jet shapes and acceleration. Nokhrina et al. (2026).

Scope of this diagram

This is a steady, axisymmetric teaching example with arbitrary intensity and opacity normalization. A constant Doppler factor and redshift are absorbed into that normalization. It omits beam convolution, time variability, polarization, radiative cooling, and detailed particle cutoffs. Free-free absorption in surrounding thermal gas and standing shocks can also influence real radio cores. Its locations are independent of the core-distance sliders in the binary model above; no fit to 3C 66B is implied.

Recent literature and evidence · reviewed 22 October 2026
Liu et al. (2026) · BL Lacertae

Multifrequency astrometry measured a shift reaching 250 μas between 8.4 and 43.2 GHz. Their kᵣ ≈ 1.18, with substantial uncertainty, is consistent with the canonical opacity model.

Nokhrina et al. (2026) · generalized jet scalings

Their self-absorbed synchrotron framework allows different jet shapes, plasma and field gradients, and Doppler-factor evolution. An inverse-frequency shift is not imposed on every AGN.

Pérez-Díez et al. (2025; arXiv 2026) · 1803+784

A measured kᵣ ≈ 0.73 challenges a simple conical equipartition interpretation for this source. The gradient controls explore how a different core-shift slope can arise.
